# Campus Photo Guide (for the owner)

Your photos are the single biggest thing that makes the game's buildings look like the real UIC.
You don't need any special camera. A recent phone is perfect.

## Before you go

- **Pick an overcast day** (cloudy, no strong shadows). Sunny days put hard shadows on walls, and those shadows get "baked" into the game's textures, which looks wrong when the game's own sun moves.
- **Mid-morning to mid-afternoon**, to avoid orange sunrise or sunset light.
- **Phone camera settings:**
  - Use the normal 1× lens. Avoid the ultra-wide 0.5× lens, because it bends straight lines.
  - Turn off HDR "vivid" or beauty filters if you can.
  - Use the highest resolution.
- **Avoid people.** Wait for a gap, or take the shot again. We won't use photos with recognizable faces.
- **Skip logos.** Don't make UIC logos, banners or restaurant signs the subject. They're trademarked and we won't put them in the game.

## Part 1: Building walls ("facade photos")

For **each building on the list below**:

1. **Stand straight in front of each side of the building**, as square-on as you can, far enough back to fit the whole wall (or one repeating section of it) in the frame.
2. **Take 3–5 photos per side**, stepping sideways a few steps between them so they overlap by about half.
3. **Take 2–3 close-ups (about 1–2 m away)** of:
   - the wall surface (concrete texture, brick)
   - a window
   - the entrance
   - the ground at the base of the wall
4. **Take one wide photo** showing the whole building in context. This is our reference picture.

**Buildings (most important first):**

1. University Hall: all 4 sides. Lots of close-ups of the windows and concrete.
2. Richard J. Daley Library
3. Student Center East
4. The Quad and Lecture Center buildings (A–F), including the pavement and steps
5. Behavioral Sciences Building
6. ARC residence hall
7. Science & Engineering Offices (SEO)
8. Student Recreation Facility
9. UIC-Halsted Blue Line station entrances
10. Any building you personally care about (your classes, your study spot)

## Part 2: Walk-around videos (for 3D photo-scans of hero spots)

For University Hall's base, the Quad, Daley Library's entrance and the SCE entrance:

1. **Record video at 4K, 30 fps.** Tap and hold on the building to **lock exposure and focus** (AE/AF lock).
2. **Walk slowly in a full circle** around the spot, about as fast as a slow museum walk. Keep the building in the middle of the frame.
3. **Do 2 loops:** one at normal eye level, and one holding the phone up high (arm raised) and tilted slightly down.
4. **Each loop takes about 1–3 minutes.** No zooming, no fast turns.

## Part 3: Materials and small details (5 minutes, anywhere)

Take close-up photos, about 1 m away, of:

- **Surfaces:** concrete walls, brick, sidewalk, asphalt, grass, a granite or stone path
- **Small objects:** a bench, a lamp post, a trash can, a bike rack, a tree trunk, a CTA bus-stop sign. Take each from 2–3 angles.

## Sending them to me

1. Put everything in one **Google Drive folder**, with a sub-folder per building, and send me the link.
2. Make sure the folder is shared so I can open it.
3. Videos are big, so upload them on Wi-Fi.

Don't worry about perfection. Even a rough first batch of University Hall photos lets the team start.
